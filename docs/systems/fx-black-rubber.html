The Problem: Moisture Destroys Structures from the Inside Out

Grain elevators, steel storage bins, and concrete foundations across Southwestern Ontario face a relentless enemy — water. Rain penetrates aging concrete joints. Condensation forms on steel surfaces during temperature swings. Snowmelt wicks through porous substrates. Once moisture enters, the damage compounds: steel corrodes, concrete spalls from freeze-thaw cycles, stored grain develops mould, and discharge systems clog with swollen product. A single season of unaddressed moisture intrusion can cost tens of thousands of dollars in spoiled inventory and emergency structural repairs.

Traditional waterproofing methods — sheet membranes, coal tar coatings, and asphalt-based products — create seams, require hot application, and become brittle in cold weather. In Southwestern Ontario's climate, where temperatures swing from +35°C in summer to -25°C in winter, rigid waterproofing fails at the worst possible time.

The Solution: FX Black Rubber

FX Black Rubber is a liquid-applied synthetic rubber membrane that cures into a seamless, flexible, permanently bonded waterproof barrier. Applied as a thick liquid coating, it conforms to every surface contour — bolt heads, rivet lines, lap joints, cracks, and irregular geometry that sheet membranes cannot follow. Once cured, FX Black Rubber remains flexible across the full temperature range of Ontario's climate, expanding and contracting with the substrate without cracking, peeling, or losing adhesion.

The system is applied using airless spray equipment or roller in multiple passes, building to a recommended dry film thickness of 40–60 mils. Each pass chemically bonds to the previous layer, creating a monolithic membrane with no seams, laps, or fastener penetrations. The result is a continuous waterproof envelope that stops moisture intrusion at the surface — before it can reach the structural steel or stored contents inside.

Built for Grain Storage and Agricultural Infrastructure

FX Black Rubber was developed specifically for the types of structures common in grain elevator and agricultural operations. Concrete bin walls develop hairline cracks from settling and thermal movement — FX Black Rubber bridges cracks up to 1/8" wide. Steel bin roofs corrode at fastener points and lap joints — the liquid membrane seals every penetration point without additional flashing. Foundation walls exposed to grade-level moisture get a continuous barrier that prevents water wicking into the structure above.

Beyond grain storage, FX Black Rubber protects roof systems on manufacturing facilities, pump house foundations at wastewater treatment plants, and secondary containment structures where waterproofing integrity is mandated by regulation. The system's ability to prevent mould growth and clog formation makes it particularly valuable in operations where product quality depends on keeping moisture out of storage environments.

Pairing with FX Cool Roof for Maximum Lifespan

While FX Black Rubber provides exceptional waterproofing, direct UV exposure accelerates degradation of any rubber-based membrane. For surfaces with significant sun exposure — particularly horizontal roofs and bin tops — FX Black Rubber is specified as the waterproofing base and topped with FX Cool Roof, a white polyurethane coating that reflects UV radiation and reduces surface temperatures by up to 30°C. This two-system approach extends the total membrane service life to 20+ years and significantly reduces interior temperatures in storage structures during summer months.

For steel surfaces that require corrosion protection beyond waterproofing, FX Steel-Shield provides a complementary epoxy and polyurethane system designed for direct-to-metal application on structural steel, I-beams, and equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can FX Black Rubber be applied in cold weather?

FX Black Rubber is formulated for all-season application and can be applied at temperatures as low as 0°C (32°F) on dry substrates. The material remains flexible after curing at temperatures down to -40°C, making it ideal for Southwestern Ontario's freeze-thaw climate. Application in rain or on wet surfaces is not recommended.

How does FX Black Rubber prevent grain spoilage?

Grain spoilage starts with moisture intrusion through cracked concrete or corroded steel. FX Black Rubber creates a continuous, monolithic membrane that seals the structure against rain, snowmelt, and condensation. By eliminating moisture pathways, the system prevents mould growth, grain clumping, and clogged discharge systems that lead to costly downtime and product loss.

Can FX Black Rubber be top-coated for UV protection?

Yes. For structures with direct sun exposure, FX Black Rubber is commonly top-coated with FX Cool Roof — a white polyurethane coating that reflects UV radiation, reduces surface temperatures, and extends the membrane lifespan by 10–15 years. This combination is the standard specification for grain elevator roofs and steel bin tops.
