Wet Floors, Heavy Traffic, and Slip-and-Fall Risk. FX Quartz Solves All Three.

Slip-and-fall incidents are the leading cause of workplace injury in industrial and commercial facilities. When your floors get wet from process water, cleaning protocols, or condensation — and when forklifts, pallet jacks, and foot traffic share the same surface — you need a coating that delivers genuine traction, not just a smooth sealed finish. Standard epoxy alone does not provide the anti-slip performance that high-risk environments demand.

FX Quartz is a multi-layer epoxy flooring system that incorporates a full broadcast of natural quartz aggregate into the coating matrix. The result is a thick, dense, textured surface that provides consistent anti-slip performance even when wet, oily, or contaminated. It handles everything from pedestrian traffic to loaded forklifts, and it does it for years without degrading.

System Build-Up and Application

FX Quartz begins with mechanical surface preparation to SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 standards. Diamond grinding or shot blasting creates the concrete surface profile (CSP 3 to 5) necessary for optimal adhesion. Following substrate preparation, a penetrating epoxy primer is applied to seal the concrete and establish the bond.

The body coat is where FX Quartz distinguishes itself. A high-build epoxy resin is applied, and while still wet, natural quartz aggregate is broadcast to full rejection — meaning the surface is completely saturated with quartz granules. After cure, loose aggregate is removed, and a seal coat of high-solids epoxy is applied to lock the quartz in place and fill the interstitial spaces. A second seal coat may be applied for chemical resistance or aesthetic requirements.

The finished system builds to 60 to 125 mils — three to five times the thickness of a standard epoxy coating. This thickness, combined with the hardness of the quartz aggregate, creates a surface that distributes point loads, resists impact, and maintains its textured profile under sustained heavy traffic.

Anti-Slip Performance That Lasts

Many anti-slip coatings lose their traction profile within months as the surface wears smooth under traffic. FX Quartz avoids this problem because the quartz aggregate is embedded throughout the full depth of the coating — not just sprinkled on top. As the surface wears, fresh aggregate is continuously exposed, maintaining slip resistance over the full service life of the system.

The degree of texture and slip resistance can be engineered by selecting different quartz grain sizes. Finer aggregates (20/40 mesh) create a smoother texture suitable for areas where bare-skin contact is common. Coarser aggregates (12/20 mesh) provide aggressive traction for wet processing areas and loading docks where boots and heavy equipment dominate.

Where FX Quartz Excels

For environments with extreme thermal shock or aggressive chemical exposure beyond what epoxy can handle, FX Shield (polyurethane concrete) offers the next level of protection. For walls and vertical surfaces that need similar durability, consider FX Wall-Glaze.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes FX Quartz anti-slip?

FX Quartz uses a full broadcast of natural quartz aggregate embedded into the epoxy matrix. The quartz granules create a textured surface profile that provides consistent traction even when wet, oily, or contaminated. The degree of slip resistance can be adjusted by selecting different quartz grain sizes.

Can FX Quartz handle forklift traffic?

Yes. FX Quartz is engineered for light, medium, and heavy traffic including forklift and pallet jack operations. The quartz aggregate broadcast creates a thick, dense coating system (60 to 125 mils) that distributes point loads and resists abrasion from rubber tires and steel wheels.

How does FX Quartz compare to FX Epoxy?

FX Quartz is a heavier-duty system than FX Epoxy. While FX Epoxy provides a smooth, sealed surface at 15 to 25 mils, FX Quartz builds to 60 to 125 mils with embedded quartz aggregate. This makes FX Quartz better suited for high-traffic areas, wet environments, and spaces where anti-slip performance is critical.
