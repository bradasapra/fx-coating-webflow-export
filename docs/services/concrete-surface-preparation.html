Sixty to Eighty Percent of All Coating Failures Start with Inadequate Surface Preparation. The Coating Was Never the Problem.

When a floor coating delaminates, blisters, or peels within the first year, the instinct is to blame the product. But in the vast majority of cases, the coating chemistry was sound. The failure was in the substrate. Laitance left on the surface. Curing compounds not removed. Contaminants — oils, grease, chemical residues — sealed under the new coating. An inadequate surface profile that gave the coating nothing to grip.

This is why FX Coating treats surface preparation as a distinct service — not a line item buried in the coating quote. Every square foot of concrete we coat is prepared to SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 standards, verified with profile measurements, and documented before any coating material touches the floor.

What SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 Requires

SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 is the industry standard for surface preparation of concrete prior to coating. Published jointly by the Society for Protective Coatings (SSPC) and NACE International (now AMPP), the standard requires that all of the following be achieved before coating application:

  • Sound concrete exposed: All laitance, weak surface layers, and deteriorated concrete must be removed to expose aggregate and sound cement paste
  • Contaminants removed: Oil, grease, wax, curing compounds, form release agents, paint, and chemical residues must be eliminated — not just cleaned, but removed from the pore structure of the concrete
  • Existing coatings removed or verified: Previous coatings must be fully removed unless adhesion testing confirms they are sound and compatible with the new system
  • Proper surface profile achieved: The concrete must be mechanically profiled to the CSP (Concrete Surface Profile) required by the specific coating system being installed
  • Moisture content verified: Relative humidity within the slab must be within the coating manufacturer's tolerance — typically below 75 percent RH at 40 percent depth for epoxy systems

Concrete Surface Profiles: CSP-3 Through CSP-7

The International Concrete Repair Institute (ICRI) defines Concrete Surface Profile on a scale from CSP-1 (nearly smooth, like a trowel finish) to CSP-10 (very rough, exposing large aggregate). Different coating systems require different profiles for proper mechanical adhesion:

CSP Requirements by FX Coating System

CSP-3Thin-film coatings, sealers — light texture comparable to fine sandpaper
CSP-3 to CSP-4FX Epoxy, FX Flakes, FX Quartz Decorative — standard epoxy and decorative systems
CSP-4 to CSP-5FX Quartz, FX Solid-Thane — heavier-build quartz broadcast and polyaspartic systems
CSP-5 to CSP-7FX Shield — polyurethane concrete requires aggressive profiling for its 6 to 9 mm build
CSP-3 to CSP-5FX Wall Armor, FX Wall-Glaze — wall coating systems (vertical surfaces prepared by abrasive methods)

Applying a coating system over an under-profiled surface means the coating cannot achieve mechanical interlock with the substrate. It sits on top rather than bonding into the concrete matrix. The result is delamination — sometimes within weeks, sometimes within the first thermal cycle or chemical exposure event.

Preparation Methods

Shot blasting is the primary preparation method for horizontal concrete surfaces. Steel shot is propelled at high velocity against the concrete surface, removing laitance, contaminants, and weak material while creating a uniform profile. Shot blasting is fast, dust-controlled (the equipment captures and recycles shot and dust), and produces profiles from CSP-3 to CSP-7 depending on machine speed and shot size. FX Coating uses self-contained shot blasting equipment on projects from 500 to 100,000+ square feet.

Scarification uses rotating cutting wheels to remove concrete material aggressively. It is the method of choice when thick existing coatings, heavy contamination, or deteriorated concrete must be removed before profiling. Scarifiers can remove 3 to 6 mm of material per pass and produce CSP-5 to CSP-8 profiles. For severely damaged slabs or slabs with multiple layers of old coatings, scarification followed by shot blasting achieves both material removal and a uniform final profile.

Diamond grinding uses rotating diamond-segmented tooling to level and smooth the concrete surface. While it produces a lower profile (CSP-1 to CSP-3), diamond grinding is essential for preparing concrete that has surface irregularities, lippage between slab sections, or high spots that would cause coating thickness variation. It is often used as a secondary step after shot blasting to achieve final flatness specifications.

Acid etching is not used by FX Coating for industrial projects. While acid etching is common in residential and light commercial work, it does not produce a consistent profile, leaves chemical residues in the concrete pore structure, creates hazardous waste, and cannot remove existing coatings or curing compounds. SSPC-SP13 permits acid etching only when mechanical methods are not feasible — a situation that essentially never arises in our industrial work.

Pre-Preparation Testing

Before any mechanical preparation begins, FX Coating performs three critical substrate tests:

  • Moisture vapour transmission testing — calcium chloride test (ASTM F1869) or relative humidity probe test (ASTM F2170) to determine if the slab's moisture levels are within the coating system's tolerance
  • Adhesion pull testing — if existing coatings are present, we test their bond strength to determine whether overlay or full removal is required
  • Contamination assessment — visual and chemical testing for oils, greases, chemical residues, and other contaminants that could compromise adhesion even after mechanical profiling

These tests determine the preparation approach, identify potential issues before they become coating failures, and ensure that the project specification matches the actual substrate conditions.

Every FX Coating System Requires Proper Preparation

Surface preparation is not a separate service from coating installation — it is the first phase of every coating project. Whether we are installing FX Shield in a food processing plant, FX Black Rubber on a grain bin, FX Wall Armor on processing room walls, or FX Steel-Shield on structural steel, the preparation standard is the same: SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 compliance, verified profile, documented results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 surface preparation?

SSPC-SP13/NACE 6 is the joint standard published by the Society for Protective Coatings (SSPC) and NACE International that defines the requirements for surface preparation of concrete prior to coating application. It specifies that the concrete surface must be sound, clean, and profiled to the level required by the coating manufacturer. This means removing all laitance, curing compounds, form oils, existing coatings, contaminants, and unsound concrete to expose a clean, profiled substrate that the new coating can mechanically bond to.

Why does surface preparation matter for coating performance?

Surface preparation is the single most important factor in coating longevity. Industry data consistently shows that 60 to 80 percent of all coating failures can be traced back to inadequate surface preparation. Without proper profiling, even premium coating systems like FX Shield or FX Epoxy cannot achieve mechanical adhesion to the substrate. The coating may look fine initially, but will delaminate under thermal cycling, chemical exposure, or moisture vapour transmission within months.

What is a Concrete Surface Profile (CSP) and which level do I need?

Concrete Surface Profile (CSP) is a standardized measurement of the roughness or texture of a prepared concrete surface, defined by ICRI on a scale from CSP-1 (nearly smooth) to CSP-10 (very rough). Different coating systems require different profiles: thin-film sealers need CSP-1 to CSP-2, standard epoxy systems like FX Epoxy require CSP-3 to CSP-5, and heavy-build polyurethane concrete systems like FX Shield require CSP-5 to CSP-7. FX Coating specifies and verifies the correct CSP for every system we install.

How long does concrete surface preparation take?

Timelines depend on area size, existing surface condition, and the required profile. For a clean, uncoated concrete slab, shot blasting can prepare 3,000 to 5,000 square feet per day. If the slab has existing coatings, heavy contamination, or deteriorated concrete requiring scarification, the process takes longer. FX Coating provides detailed preparation timelines as part of every project quote.
