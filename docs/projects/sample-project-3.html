St. Marys Water Treatment — Administrative Floor System

Moisture-Tolerant Urethane Cement with Integrated Cove Base for a Seamless, Wash-Down Ready Environment

The Challenge

Water treatment facilities present a paradox for floor coatings. The administrative and operational areas need professional, durable flooring — but the environmental conditions inside a water treatment plant actively destroy most coating systems. The St. Marys Water Treatment plant needed an administrative flooring solution that could withstand far more than standard foot traffic.

The primary technical challenge was moisture vapor transmission (MVT). Water treatment facilities, by their nature, have high ambient moisture. The concrete slabs in these buildings are often in contact with saturated ground, and the building environment itself adds humidity from open water processes. This elevated MVT is the single most common cause of coating failure in institutional facilities — the moisture migrates through the concrete slab, reaches the underside of the coating, and creates hydraulic pressure that causes bubbling and delamination.

Previous coating systems at the facility had failed for exactly this reason. Standard commercial epoxy coatings were applied, looked excellent for a few months, then began bubbling and peeling as moisture vapor pushed up through the slab. Each failure cycle meant disruption, removal of the failed coating, and another attempt — an expensive and frustrating pattern.

A secondary but significant challenge was the standard baseboard joints where the floor met the walls. These 90-degree joints created "dirt traps" that were difficult to sanitize. In a water treatment environment where cleanliness and hygiene matter, these joints accumulated moisture, debris, and microbial growth that could not be effectively cleaned with standard mopping or wash-down procedures.

The FX Solution

FX Coating engineered a hybrid "Asset Defense" strategy using FX Shield — a multi-layer system designed to solve both the moisture vapor transmission problem and the baseboard hygiene problem in a single installation.

The installation followed a precisely sequenced protocol:

  1. Shot blasting to CSP-5 profile: The entire floor was mechanically shot blasted to achieve a Concrete Surface Profile of CSP-5 — an aggressive profile that creates deep mechanical anchoring points for the coating system. This profile is essential for permanent bond in high-MVT environments, because the coating system locks into the concrete texture rather than relying solely on chemical adhesion that moisture can undermine.
  2. Moisture-tolerant base — Tnemec N242 Polyurethane Concrete: The base layer of the system was Tnemec N242, a polyurethane concrete specifically formulated to tolerate elevated moisture vapor transmission. Unlike standard epoxy primers that form a rigid barrier that moisture pressure eventually breaks, N242 is moisture-tolerant by chemistry — it accommodates moisture migration without delaminating. This layer provided the structural foundation of the floor system.
  3. Full broadcast Industrial Sand Quartz: While the N242 base was still tacky, the FX Coating team broadcast Industrial Sand Quartz aggregate across the entire surface. This full broadcast serves two purposes: it creates a high-traction safety surface that exceeds slip-resistance requirements for institutional environments, and it creates a rough texture that provides maximum mechanical bond for the topcoat layer above.
  4. Chemical-resistant topcoat — Tnemec N222 in 33GR Gray: The final wearing surface was Tnemec N222, a chemical-resistant topcoat applied in 33GR Gray. This topcoat provides the chemical resistance, abrasion resistance, and UV stability required for a long-service-life institutional floor. The 33GR Gray color was selected for the administrative environment — professional, clean, and practical for showing cleanliness without showing every footprint.
  5. Integrated Rolled Radius Cove: The critical finishing detail was the installation of a rolled radius cove that curves 4 inches up the wall from the floor surface. This eliminated the standard 90-degree baseboard joint entirely. The cove creates a smooth, curved transition from floor to wall — a completely seamless, wash-down ready junction with no joints, no edges, and no dirt traps. Water, cleaning solution, and debris flow smoothly from floor to wall during wash-down rather than accumulating in corners.

The Results

The FX Shield system at St. Marys Water Treatment solved both problems that had plagued the facility. The moisture-tolerant Tnemec N242 base eliminated the bubbling and delamination cycle that had defeated every previous coating attempt. The system achieved permanent adhesion despite the high moisture vapor transmission in the slab — because the polyurethane concrete chemistry was engineered to tolerate that moisture rather than being destroyed by it.

The integrated rolled radius cove transformed the floor-to-wall junction from a hygiene liability into a cleanable, seamless surface. Maintenance staff reported that the cove areas, which had previously required manual scrubbing to remove accumulated debris, now cleaned as easily as the open floor surface. The entire room became a wash-down ready environment — a single continuous surface from floor to wall with no joints or seams where contaminants could hide.

The Industrial Sand Quartz broadcast provided a high-traction surface that addresses safety requirements in a facility where water and cleaning chemicals are regularly present on the floor. The Tnemec N222 topcoat in 33GR Gray gave the administrative area a professional, institutional appearance that communicates the quality and cleanliness expected in a municipal facility.

For the Town of St. Marys, the FX Shield system ended the cycle of coating failure and replacement. The floor is now a permanent asset — a 15 to 25 year coating system that will not bubble, delaminate, or peel regardless of moisture conditions in the slab.
