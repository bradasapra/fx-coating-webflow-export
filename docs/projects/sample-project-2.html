Oil-Tolerant Epoxy System for a Contaminated Hangar Floor

The Challenge

Airplane hangars subject concrete floors to a unique combination of abuse. Heavy aircraft roll across the surface on hard rubber tires. Hydraulic fluid, engine oil, and aviation fuel drip and pool on the floor during maintenance. De-icing chemicals are tracked in during winter operations. And the floor must remain clean enough for technicians to work safely — oil-slicked concrete is a slip hazard and a liability.

At Brant Aero in Brantford, Ontario, the airplane hangar faced recurring floor contamination from oil leaks that were hard to clean and impossible to coat with standard products. Previous attempts to apply conventional floor coatings had failed. The oil had penetrated deep into the concrete pore structure over years of use, and standard epoxy primers could not achieve adhesion on the contaminated substrate. Each coating attempt would look good initially, then begin peeling and chipping within months as the oil migrated to the surface and broke the bond.

The facility needed a floor coating system that could achieve permanent adhesion on oil-contaminated concrete — not a cosmetic fix that would fail again in a year.

The FX Solution

FX Coating specified FX Epoxy with a multi-stage surface preparation protocol designed specifically for oil-contaminated substrates. The standard approach of simply grinding the surface and applying primer would not work here — the oil contamination required a more aggressive strategy.

The restoration process followed a carefully sequenced protocol:

  1. Hot water degreasing: The first step was to remove as much surface oil and grease as possible using hot water degreasing. This lifted the mobile oil fraction from the concrete pores — the fresh and semi-fresh contamination that was actively migrating through the slab.
  2. Mechanical shot blasting: After degreasing, the entire floor was mechanically shot blasted. This removed the top layer of contaminated concrete, exposing a cleaner substrate and creating the mechanical profile (CSP-3 to CSP-5) required for proper coating adhesion. Shot blasting also opened the concrete pore structure, allowing the oil-tolerant primer to penetrate effectively.
  3. Oil-tolerant primer application: Rather than using a standard epoxy primer that would lose adhesion as residual oil migrated to the surface, FX Coating applied an oil-tolerant primer specifically formulated to bond to substrates with residual hydrocarbon contamination. This primer penetrates into the concrete pore structure and cures to form a mechanical and chemical bond that is not disrupted by the presence of oil.
  4. High-performance topcoat: Over the primed surface, FX Coating applied a high-performance, oil-resistant FX Epoxy topcoat. This topcoat was selected for its resistance to aviation oil, hydraulic fluid, and fuel — the specific chemicals that the hangar floor would be exposed to on a daily basis.

The Results

The completed floor at Brant Aero delivered what previous coating attempts had not: a clean, safe, durable surface that resists chipping and oil intrusion. The oil-tolerant primer system solved the adhesion problem that had defeated standard coatings, and the FX Epoxy topcoat provides the chemical resistance and mechanical durability that a working airplane hangar demands.

The floor is now easy to clean — oil spills wipe up rather than soaking into the concrete. The surface resists chipping from dropped tools and equipment. And the coating system maintains its adhesion even in areas where oil exposure is heaviest, because the primer was designed to coexist with residual hydrocarbons rather than being destroyed by them.

For Brant Aero, the FX Epoxy system transformed the hangar from a facility with a perpetual floor problem into a professional, clean working environment. The floor coating is no longer a maintenance headache — it is a durable, long-term asset.

"We had tried coating the floor twice before and both times it peeled up within a year. FX Coating took a completely different approach — the degreasing, shot blasting, and oil-tolerant primer made all the difference. The floor has held up through a full season of maintenance work and still looks like the day it was installed."

— David Chen, Facility Manager, Brant Aero
