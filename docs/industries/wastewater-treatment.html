Your Treatment Plant Infrastructure Is Under Chemical Attack 24 Hours a Day

Wastewater treatment facilities operate in one of the most chemically aggressive environments in any industry. Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) gas — generated by anaerobic decomposition of organic material in collection systems, headworks, and primary treatment stages — attacks concrete and steel structures with a corrosion mechanism that no standard paint system can withstand. H2S rises from the water surface, oxidizes to sulfuric acid on moist concrete and steel, and dissolves the calcium hydroxide binder that holds concrete together. The result is biogenic sulfuric acid corrosion that can consume 5 to 10 millimetres of concrete per year in severe cases, turning structural members into crumbling paste.

Below the waterline, concrete tanks and channels face direct chemical attack from the wastewater itself — a constantly changing mixture of organic acids, industrial discharges, chlorides, and abrasive suspended solids. Above the waterline, the vapour space concentrates H2S and ammonia that corrode steel handrails, grating, pipe supports, and clarifier mechanisms. Secondary containment areas around chemical storage and dosing systems must resist concentrated sulfuric acid, sodium hypochlorite, ferric chloride, and polymer solutions — any one of which will destroy an unprotected concrete surface.

The cost of deferred maintenance in wastewater infrastructure is measured in structural failure, regulatory non-compliance, and emergency capital expenditure. A proactive coating and lining program extends the service life of concrete and steel assets by decades and prevents the cascade of failures that begins when corrosion compromises structural integrity.

Concrete Protection: Floors, Walls, and Containment Structures

The primary threat to concrete in wastewater treatment is biogenic sulfuric acid corrosion in the vapour space and direct chemical attack below the waterline. Different structures require different coating strategies based on their exposure conditions.

FX Shield polyurethane concrete is specified for floors in chemical storage areas, dosing rooms, pump galleries, and administrative areas where chemical resistance must combine with mechanical durability. The 6 to 9 mm monolithic floor withstands concentrated acids, caustics, and solvents while handling forklift traffic, chemical cart wheels, and heavy foot traffic. Its moisture tolerance makes it suitable for application on slabs with elevated moisture vapour transmission — a near-universal condition in wastewater facilities.

FX Black Rubber spray-applied membrane is the primary system for walls, ceilings, and containment structures exposed to H2S vapour and chemical splash. Applied at 40 to 80 mils thickness, it creates a seamless barrier that prevents acid from reaching the concrete substrate. For secondary containment berms and sumps, FX Black Rubber provides the chemical resistance and impermeability required by Ontario Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks (MECP) regulations for containment integrity.

For interior walls of process buildings, control rooms, and laboratory spaces where chemical exposure is lower but washdown and hygiene are required, FX Wall-Glaze provides a smooth, cleanable wall surface that resists moisture and mild chemical exposure.

Structural Steel Protection

Structural steel in wastewater treatment plants faces corrosion from H2S, high humidity, chemical splash, and UV exposure on outdoor structures. Handrails, grating supports, pipe bridges, clarifier mechanisms, weir plates, and building steel all require protective coating systems matched to their specific exposure conditions.

FX Steel-Shield provides a three-coat system engineered for these conditions: a zinc-rich primer for cathodic protection, a high-build epoxy intermediate coat for barrier protection, and a chemical-resistant topcoat for UV stability and surface defence. The system is applied over surfaces prepared to SSPC-SP6 or SSPC-SP10 depending on the severity of the exposure environment. Service life is 15 to 20 years on properly prepared substrates — significantly exceeding the 3 to 5 year maintenance cycle typical of single-coat alkyd systems.

Tank and Channel Linings

Interior linings for clarifiers, aeration basins, digesters, chemical tanks, and distribution channels require specialized systems that resist both immersion exposure and the abrasion of suspended solids. FX Coating applies high-build epoxy and polyurethane lining systems from manufacturers including Tnemec, Sika, and Carboline — selecting the system based on the specific chemical exposure, temperature range, and immersion conditions of each structure. All lining work follows SSPC and NACE application standards, with holiday testing and adhesion verification on every installation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What coatings resist hydrogen sulfide (H2S) corrosion in wastewater facilities?

Hydrogen sulfide converts to sulfuric acid on moist concrete surfaces, dissolving the calcium hydroxide binder and turning sound concrete into paste within years. FX Shield polyurethane concrete and FX Black Rubber membrane systems both provide verified resistance to H2S-induced biogenic sulfuric acid corrosion. FX Shield is used for floors and containment areas where mechanical and chemical resistance are both required. FX Black Rubber is used for walls, ceilings, and structures where waterproofing and acid resistance must work together. Both systems are applied over properly prepared substrates following SSPC-SP13 standards.

Can you line secondary containment areas to meet Ontario regulatory requirements?

Yes. FX Coating installs chemical-resistant linings in secondary containment berms, sumps, trenches, and tank surrounds. FX Black Rubber provides a seamless membrane that prevents chemical migration into soil and groundwater. For containment areas exposed to concentrated chemicals, FX Shield polyurethane concrete provides a thick, monolithic floor that resists acids, caustics, and solvents while supporting vehicle traffic. All containment lining work is performed to meet Ontario Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks (MECP) requirements for secondary containment integrity.

How do you protect structural steel in wastewater treatment environments?

FX Steel-Shield is a high-build epoxy coating system for structural steel in corrosive environments. The system includes a zinc-rich primer that provides cathodic protection, an intermediate high-build epoxy layer, and a chemical-resistant topcoat. This three-coat approach protects handrails, grating supports, pipe bridges, clarifier mechanisms, and other steel components from the H2S, moisture, and chemical exposure that define wastewater treatment atmospheres. Service life is 15 to 20 years depending on exposure severity.

What surface preparation is required before coating wastewater infrastructure?

Wastewater infrastructure typically requires aggressive surface preparation due to chemical contamination and deterioration of the existing substrate. Concrete surfaces are prepared to SSPC-SP13 / NACE 6 using shot blasting or scarification to achieve the required profile and remove contaminated surface material. Steel surfaces are prepared to SSPC-SP6 (commercial blast) or SSPC-SP10 (near-white blast) depending on the coating system specified. All surface preparation follows NACE and SSPC standards and is verified with profile measurements before coating application begins.
