Your Floors and Walls Are the Weakest Link in Your Food Safety Program

Food processing facilities in Ontario face a daily assault that most coatings cannot survive: steam cleaning at 80 to 100 degrees Celsius, caustic wash-down chemicals at concentrations designed to kill pathogens, organic acids from meat, dairy, and fermentation byproducts, and heavy forklift traffic over wet, contaminated surfaces. Every shift. Every day. If your floor coating cracks, delaminates, or develops micro-pores, those openings become reservoirs for Listeria, Salmonella, and E. coli — invisible contamination points that jeopardize your entire production run.

Walls present the same risk. Painted concrete block and traditional tile develop cracks and grout joints that harbour bacteria in spaces no chemical sanitizer can reach. Every washdown cycle drives moisture and organic material deeper into these crevices. The surface looks clean, but the substrate beneath is colonized — a ticking clock until your next CFIA audit identifies non-conformance, or worse, until a contamination event reaches your finished product.

FX Coating installs complete room encapsulation systems — floors, walls, and coved transitions — that eliminate the surface deficiencies where contamination takes hold. Every system we specify for food processing meets CFIA and USDA requirements for incidental food contact, withstands the thermal shock and chemical exposure of industrial washdown protocols, and delivers a service life measured in decades rather than months.

Floors: Thermal Shock Resistance That Standard Epoxy Cannot Match

The fundamental problem with conventional epoxy flooring in food processing is thermal cycling. When hot washdown water at 80 to 100 degrees Celsius hits a concrete slab at 15 to 20 degrees, the thin epoxy film (0.3 to 3 mm) cannot accommodate the differential expansion. Micro-cracks develop within months. Once cracked, moisture migrates from below through the slab and attacks the bond line from underneath. Within 12 to 24 months, the coating is delaminating in sheets.

FX Shield eliminates this failure mode. As a polyurethane concrete system applied at 6 to 9 mm thickness, it tolerates thermal shock from -40 to +120 degrees Celsius without cracking or losing adhesion. The polyurethane binder flexes with the substrate during thermal cycling instead of fighting it. FX Shield's moisture tolerance means it bonds permanently even on slabs with elevated moisture vapour transmission — the condition that causes most epoxy failures in food plants built on grade without effective vapour barriers.

FX Shield is CFIA and USDA compliant for incidental food contact. The cured system is non-toxic, non-tainting, and produces no odour transfer to food products. It resists the full spectrum of food processing chemicals: caustic CIP solutions, peracetic acid sanitizers, chlorinated cleaners, organic acids from meat and dairy, and concentrated sugar solutions.

Walls: Eliminating Bacterial Harbourage Points

The walls of a food processing room accumulate moisture, chemical residue, and organic matter from production and washdown operations. Painted concrete block — the most common wall finish in Ontario food plants — has a porous, textured surface with mortar joints every 200 mm. Each joint and each surface pore is a potential harbourage point for pathogenic bacteria.

FX Wall Armor replaces these deficient surfaces with a fiberglass-reinforced panel installed directly over existing block, poured concrete, or deteriorated finishes. The panels create a structural, non-porous surface with no joints, no crevices, and no surface texture for bacteria to colonize. A finish coat of FX Solid-Thane adds a non-stick urethane surface that prevents organic material from bonding to the wall — reducing cleaning time by up to 50 percent.

For applications where a lighter-duty wall coating is appropriate — control rooms, packaging areas, dry storage — FX Wall-Glaze provides a smooth, seamless coating over existing surfaces that meets CFIA hygiene standards without the structural reinforcement of the fiberglass panel system.

Coved Transitions: Completing the Room Encapsulation

The 90-degree junction where floors meet walls is the single most common contamination point in food processing rooms. Water, chemicals, and organic debris pool in this angle, and no amount of washdown pressure can fully clean a sharp inside corner. CFIA, GFSI, and BRC auditors consistently flag uncoated floor-wall junctions as non-conformances.

FX Cove creates a smooth, radiused transition that curves the floor coating 100 mm (4 inches) up the wall surface. The cove integrates with both the floor system below and the wall coating above, creating a continuous, seamless surface that eliminates the contamination trap entirely. Combined with FX Shield on the floor and FX Wall Armor or FX Wall-Glaze on the walls, FX Cove completes a full room encapsulation that meets the highest food safety standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

What floor coating meets CFIA requirements for food processing plants?

FX Shield polyurethane concrete is CFIA and USDA compliant for incidental food contact. The cured system is non-toxic, non-tainting, and produces no odour transfer. Applied at 6 to 9 mm thickness, FX Shield withstands thermal shock from steam cleaning at 80 to 100 degrees Celsius, resists caustic and acidic wash-down chemicals, and handles heavy forklift traffic on wet surfaces. It is specified for meat processing kill floors, dairy production areas, bakery production lines, and brewery bottling halls across Ontario.

How do you prevent bacterial buildup on food processing walls?

Bacteria colonize the cracks, joints, and surface texture of painted concrete block and tiled walls. FX Wall Armor replaces these porous surfaces with a non-porous fiberglass reinforced panel that has no joints or crevices. A topcoat of FX Solid-Thane creates a non-stick surface that prevents organic material from bonding to the wall, reducing cleaning time by up to 50 percent. For smooth wall surfaces without the structural reinforcement of fiberglass, FX Wall-Glaze provides a seamless, washdown-ready coating that meets CFIA hygiene standards.

Why do standard epoxy floors fail in food processing environments?

Standard epoxy coatings are thin-film systems applied at 0.3 to 3 mm. They soften under heat from steam cleaning and hot process water, they crack under thermal cycling when hot water hits cold concrete, and they lose adhesion when moisture migrates through the slab from below. In food processing environments with daily washdown at 80 to 100 degrees Celsius, conventional epoxy typically begins failing within 12 to 24 months. FX Shield polyurethane concrete is applied at 6 to 9 mm and tolerates thermal shock from -40 to +120 degrees Celsius — it is engineered for the conditions that destroy epoxy.

What is a coved floor-wall transition and why does food processing need it?

A coved transition replaces the 90-degree angle where the floor meets the wall with a smooth, curved radius. This eliminates the joint where water, chemicals, and organic debris accumulate and bacteria colonize. FX Cove creates a seamless curved transition that integrates with the floor coating and wall system, creating a fully enclosed room surface that can be washed down without trapping contaminants. Coved transitions are required or recommended by CFIA, GFSI, and most third-party food safety audit frameworks.
